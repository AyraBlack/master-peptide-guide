BPC-157
Body Protection Compound-157 · 15 amino acids · Gastric peptide
▼
Tendon repairGut healingAngiogenesisAnti-inflammatoryMuscle repair
What it is
A synthetic 15-amino-acid peptide derived from a protein in human gastric juice. Stable in stomach acid. Promotes new blood vessel growth (angiogenesis), activates the nitric oxide system, and accelerates cell migration to injury sites. Works on tendons, ligaments, muscles, gut lining, bone, and even the nervous system.

Real life effects — what people actually notice
Days 1–3
Reduced pain and inflammation at injury site. Many people report this is the first thing they notice — it "takes the edge off" joint or tendon pain quite quickly.
Week 1–2
Improved range of motion and reduced stiffness. Gut issues (bloating, reflux, IBS-type symptoms) often calm down noticeably within this window.
Week 3–6
Actual tissue repair becomes apparent — tendons and ligaments that previously hurt under load start to tolerate more. Athletes report being able to train through injuries they previously couldn't.
Week 6–12
Full structural repair in many cases. Some people report injuries healed that had been stubborn for years. Mood improvements also commonly reported (dopamine/serotonin modulation).
Biohacking — what people actually use it for
Injury rescue
The #1 use. Torn ligaments, chronic tendinopathies, labrum injuries, rotator cuff problems — things that would normally need surgery or take 12+ months. People report dramatic acceleration.
Gut healing
Oral BPC-157 for IBD, Crohn's, SIBO, leaky gut, and NSAID/alcohol-induced GI damage. Extremely popular in the gut health community.
Post-surgery recovery
Used off-label to dramatically speed healing after orthopedic surgeries. Often stacked with TB-500.
Depression / mood
Increasingly used for mood disorders — it modulates dopamine and serotonin. Some report antidepressant-like effects comparable to SSRIs without the side effects.
Hangover / alcohol protection
Used to protect the GI tract and liver from alcohol damage. Some biohackers take oral BPC-157 the morning after heavy drinking.
Side effects & risks
One of the safest peptides — no LD50 established in animals. Human reports: mild nausea, light-headedness, injection site redness. Theoretical concern: promotes angiogenesis, so caution if you have existing cancer or are high-risk. Not studied long-term in humans.

Peptide 5 (Connexin 43)
Cx43 mimetic · Extracellular loop peptide · Gap junction modulator
▼
Wound healingReduced scarringAnti-arrhythmicCardiac protectionGap junction modulation
What it is
Peptide 5 is a mimetic of the second extracellular loop of Connexin 43 (Cx43) — the most abundant gap junction protein in the heart and skin. Gap junctions are channels that connect adjacent cells and allow direct communication. In damaged tissue (wounds, heart attacks, brain injury), Cx43 hemichannels open abnormally, leaking ATP and causing cell death and inflammation. Peptide 5 blocks these hemichannels, reducing damage spread. It also reduces the inflammatory signalling cascade that follows injury, thereby dramatically reducing scarring and accelerating tissue repair.
How to take it
Route (wounds)
Topical — applied directly in gel or solution
Route (cardiac/systemic)
Intravenous or subcutaneous in animal studies
Status
No established human self-use protocol — research stage
Related clinical work
αCT1 (a related Cx43 peptide) is in Phase 3 for chronic wounds
Real life effects
Wound healing
Animal studies show dramatically reduced scar formation — wounds heal with regenerative tissue rather than fibrous scar. The quality of healed tissue is markedly better. Skin regains normal architecture.
Cardiac (experimental)
After simulated heart attack, Peptide 5 reduces arrhythmia induction rate and limits the spread of cell death beyond the initial ischaemic zone. Preservation of cardiac function is significantly better.
Human anecdotal
Very limited self-use data. A niche but growing interest in the biohacking community, primarily for scar minimisation after surgery or injury.
Biohacking uses
Scar prevention
Applied topically after surgery, burns, or skin injuries to minimise scarring. Animal data shows regenerative rather than fibrous healing — a major quality-of-life difference for cosmetically sensitive areas.
Cardiac biohacking
Explored by heart health-focused biohackers for post-myocardial protection. Most experimental use case on this list — essentially frontier territory.
Neuroprotection (brain injury)
Cx43 hemichannel opening causes secondary damage after TBI and stroke. Studied for reducing this spread — used experimentally by those with TBI history.
Side effects & risks
Topical application has an excellent safety profile in studies — no significant toxicity. Systemic use is less characterised. Gap junction modulation has theoretical consequences for intercellular communication in normal tissue at high doses. One of the more specialised peptides on this list — limited self-use community data. Human safety largely inferred from the related αCT1 peptide's Phase 3 wound trial safety data.
Related clinical: αCT1 in Phase 3 (chronic wounds)
Cartalax 20mg

▼

What it is
Cartalax is a tripeptide (Ala-Glu-Asp) from Prof. Khavinson's Russian bioregulator family — the same research institute that produced Epithalon. It was derived from the alpha-1 chain of type XI collagen, a structural collagen found in cartilage. It enters cells and interacts with chromatin, modulating gene expression in chondrocytes (cartilage cells) and osteoblasts (bone cells). Key actions include: upregulating SIRT6 (a longevity gene), downregulating p53 and p21 (senescence markers), stimulating PCNA (cell proliferation marker), and suppressing MMP-9 (the enzyme that breaks down cartilage matrix).

Post-injury cartilage repair
Used after meniscus damage, labral tears, and other cartilage injuries — particularly ones where surgery is being considered or avoided. Stacked with BPC-157 for synergy.
Side effects & risks
Excellent safety profile from Khavinson's 40-year research program. No significant toxicity in cell or animal studies. Injection site reactions possible. The p53 downregulation (reducing cell death) is generally desirable in aging tissue but theoretically requires caution in cancer contexts — same caveat as Epithalon. Overall: one of the lower-risk compounds on this list.
